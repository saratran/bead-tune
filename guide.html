How to use BeadTune

BeadTune turns a photo or drawing into a fuse bead pattern you can build on pegboards, with a shopping list of beads. Its ✨ Auto suggestions try hundreds of settings and fine-tune the best, so you can pick a look instead of fiddling with sliders.

Quick start

  1. Add an image. Drop it on the page, click to choose a file, or paste it. Try the sample if you just want to look around. Your image never leaves your browser.
  2. Pick your beads and size. In Size & beads, choose the bead size and brand, how many beads wide the pattern should be, and your pegboard size.
  3. Let Auto find a look. Click ✨ Auto suggestions → Find suggestions, then Use this on the one you like. Fine-tune by hand if you want.
  4. Tidy and build. Swap or remove colours in Beads to buy, touch up beads with Edit beads, then Export a PDF or follow along in Build mode.

Tip: crop the image to just the subject first (✂ Crop). Fewer beads go into the background and the subject gets more detail.

Beads and size

Bead size
Mini 2.6 mm, Midi 5 mm (the usual Perler / Hama size) or Maxi 10 mm. The list of brands follows the size.
Beads
The brand's colour chart: MARD, Perler, Hama, Artkal or Nabbi. Every colour in the pattern is a real bead with its code.
Mix in other brands
Beads of the same size from different brands fuse together, so you can combine charts for more colours. Mixed colours show their brand in the list.
Width and pegboard size
The width is in beads; the height follows the image. 52 wide is two square 26-peg boards across (2 × 2 boards for a square image). Bigger patterns keep more detail but take more beads and time.
Only colours I have
Tick the beads you own under Choose, then switch this on to use only those.

Image settings

Everything updates the pattern straight away. You rarely need all of these — Auto tries them for you.

Sampling
Smooth blends the colours inside each bead (best for photos and paintings). Sharp takes each bead's main colour (crisper edges for drawings and logos). Pixel art detects the pixels of enlarged pixel art and copies them bead for bead.
Brightness, contrast, saturation
Bolder colours often keep faint details — a mouth, a blush — that would otherwise blend into the skin.
Background
Remove background leaves empty pegs around the subject (pick the background colour from the image if it guesses wrong). Trim empty space crops the pattern to the subject.
Colours
The most bead colours to use. Fewer colours are cheaper and easier; more keep shading. Accurate colour matching (CIEDE2000) usually picks closer beads than Standard.
Dithering
Mixes two bead colours in a pattern to suggest a shade in between — good for smooth gradients, noisy up close.
Clean up
Removes stray single beads and tiny groups, so there's less to place. Very dark or bright single beads (eyes, sparkles) are kept.

✨ Auto suggestions

Auto builds and scores hundreds of patterns from your image with different settings, then fine-tunes the best ones. It keeps your bead set, width and crop, and never removes the background.

  1. Search. Pick a preset (the default is a good start) and Colour tones: Natural aims for the original's colours, Vivid for richer ones, Muted for softer ones. Find suggestions runs it.
  2. Results. You get named picks — Most faithful, Most detailed, Balanced, Simplest, Smooth shading, Crisp — for each tone. Click a thumbnail to see it large next to the original.
  3. Make it yours. ★ bookmarks a result (kept for this image across searches and in projects). More like this fine-tunes around one result; tick Prefer on several and Tune selected to blend them. ★ Bookmark current settings adds your own hand-made settings, so you can compare and tune them too.
  4. Use it. Use this applies the settings to your pattern.

Search space and presets: open Search space to choose exactly which values are tried, and save your favourite set as a preset.

Reading the scores

Every result is scored out of 100 for this image, on the same scale for suggestions, bookmarks and your own settings:

  • Features — outlines, eyes and faint details kept. 100 is the most detail beads can keep for this image.
  • Likeness — how close the colours are to the original (for the chosen tone).
  • Ease — fewer colours and stray beads, bigger areas of one colour: quicker to build.

0 is a deliberately crude 6-colour version. Scores trade off: the most detailed pattern is rarely the easiest. A bold, vivid look can have lower likeness and still be the one you like best — trust your eyes.

Build mode

Build shows one pegboard at a time. Pick a colour to see only its beads, place them, and tap (or drag over) beads to tick them off. Board done finishes a board and moves to the next; ← → switch boards. Choose how beads are drawn (square, bead, cross or dot) and show colour codes from the top bar. Your progress is saved with the project and shows on the Build button.

Printing and fusing

Export makes a PNG or a PDF with one page per pegboard, colour codes in each peg, a grid with coordinates and the bead count.

  • Place the beads on the pegboard (Build mode or the printout), then cover them with ironing paper.
  • Iron on medium heat in small circles for 10–20 seconds, until the beads fuse but the holes stay open. Let it cool under a book so it stays flat, then fuse the back if you like.
  • Big patterns: join the boards with a strip of beads across the seams before ironing.

Projects

Save keeps the image and every setting, edit, bookmark and build progress as a project; Save as… saves a new version ("Name v2"), and versions are grouped in the list. Projects are stored in this browser. If BeadTune is self-hosted with its server, you can also save to the server to share projects between your devices.

Questions

Is my image uploaded?
No. Everything happens in your browser.
The colours look a little different from my beads.
Screens and bead batches vary. Colours come from community colour charts (maxcleme/beadcolors); check against your real beads and swap any that are off.
Auto is slow.
It uses all but one of your computer's cores. Fewer combinations (Try at most), a smaller width or the Quick preset make it faster.
